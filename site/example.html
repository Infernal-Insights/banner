Switchboard

A small group is building a client that keeps publishing when the usual routes are blocked. This page is what someone reads before they decide to contribute a computer. It is an example, not a live project.

Why contribute your computer

A build only ships if someone else can reproduce it.

Your computer compiles one part of the client, or checks a build someone else produced. A different computer does the same thing. If they agree, that part is in, and your vote in the project goes up.

That vote is how the group decides the next release, and whether the instructions for the work should change. It cannot be bought, and it cannot be handed to someone else.

  • Ship a client that does not need a company in the middle.
  • Let the people who built it decide the next release.
  • Keep every check public, so a bad build cannot be waved through.
CPU for builds GPU only if you allow it

What your computer does

One build, then it waits.

It does not run all night for its own sake. When there is no build to check, your cores are yours again. You can support a model project at the same time, and give that one the GPU instead.

On a real project, Join adds it to the app on your computer. You then pick how many cores it may use, and whether it may use a GPU. This example has nowhere to send that work yet.
